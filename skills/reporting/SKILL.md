# reporting

Write reports that help the intended reader see what matters and decide what to do, whatever the source, tool, domain, or output surface.

## Canvas boundary

Use `artifacts` for visual evidence, charts, or an authored canvas. A proof or report canvas presents the goal, method, observations, confidence, and actual review verdict. Put operational follow-ups in the owning task through `tasks`, a PR, or a handoff. Plan canvases may contain actions.

## Principles

- Lead with the conclusion or the most important change.
- Establish the audience, purpose, period, comparison, units, and relevant definitions.
- Use traceable evidence and name its source and date window.
- Separate facts, interpretation, confidence, recommendations, and limitations.
- Compare like with like. Call out definition, coverage, attribution, currency, or timing differences that affect the conclusion.
- Put material caveats and limitations beside the claims they qualify.
- Never invent missing numbers or present partial data as complete.
- When the evidence is not trustworthy enough, stop confident interpretation. State the gap and the smallest useful next check.
- Match the requested language, tone, and level of detail.
- Omit empty sections, raw data narration, and metrics that do not affect the decision.
- Use a chart or table only when it makes a comparison or decision clearer.
- Tie each recommendation to the evidence and state the next action or decision.

## Shape

Fit the structure to the ask, not a template.

- A direct answer needs the finding, evidence, confidence, and any material limitation.
- A recurring report usually needs a summary, changes, drivers, risks, and actions.
- An anomaly memo usually needs the event, scale, likely cause, confidence, and next check.

## Review

Before handing off, confirm that the period and baseline are correct, metrics are internally consistent, claims are supported, caveats are visible, and recommendations follow from the evidence. For a proof or report canvas, also check the rendered headings and body for handoff material, including renamed sections, cards, and footers, and remove it before delivery.

The report is complete when its shortest useful version gives the right reader enough context, evidence, uncertainty, and direction to act.
